# Defense v3 and yakuman handling (2026-10-18)

Goal: protect better against Novice-queue players (random-looking discards, big open hands)
and against strong players steering the hand, and stop losing yakuman. Everything below is
behind `DefenseModel.V3` (settings: "Defense model v3 (testing)"); V2 and Legacy are kept for A/B.

## 1. What was wrong

### Yakuman

`YakuDetector` and the win gate were **not** the problem. A probe (all twelve Doman yakuman,
ron and tsumo, after kans, open daisangen, 13-sided kokushi, confirmed and unconfirmed
windows) detected and declared every one. What failed was everything before the win:

| Gap | Where | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A hand that is not tenpai is worth dora + 1 han (~1 500) | `HandValue.EstimatePoints` | a yakuman 1-shanten folds to any riichi like trash |
| The tenpai budget tops out at "cut a 10 % tile" (12 %) | `PushFoldPolicy.Decide` | a 48 000 tenpai still folds a 15 % tile |
| Open hands bypass the network and rank by tile efficiency | `LearnedDiscardPolicy` → `HandAnalyzer` | East triplet + 6z pon + three wind pairs threw a wind pair to keep 5m-6m |
| Riichi on a yakuman tenpai | `RiichiPolicy` vetoes on shape only | warns the table for no extra value |
| No defence against a visible yakuman | danger model | the third dragon into two dragon sets was an ordinary honor |

The lost live win (auto play, logs rotated) could not be reconstructed; `win_offers.jsonl`
now records every offer, decision, dispatch and outcome so the next one cannot vanish.

### Defense

Live logs to 2026-09-25: 11 deal-ins in 310 hands, but three cost 12 000-13 000 — one a 19 %
half-suji pushed at turn 14 into a two-call open hand rated 72 % tenpai. 2026-10-18 (human
match): a 2-shanten worth 1 000 ran a 7 % budget against the dealer's two-call ~10 000,
loosened by the tenpai division and a ×1.39 placement factor — nothing compared what the
push could win with what it risked.

The danger model applied riichi tables to every seat, so open hands were read wrong in both
directions (below).

## 3. What V3 does

1. **Hand reads** (`HandRead`, rules identical to the counter): flush (suit), toitoi, yakuhai,
   tanyao, other. Against a non-riichi seat, danger = the riichi-table estimate (kabe, live
   suji, dora intact) × corpus rate(read, class) / corpus rate(riichi, class)
   (`OpenDangerTable.DangerRatio`, clamped 0.05-4). Dama seats use the dama row.
2. **Tsumogiri tell**: the trailing run is a likelihood ratio on the tenpai odds, applied to
   the logistic estimate and to the network's tenpai head (`TenpaiEstimator.WithTsumogiri`).
3. **Threat value**: open hands priced from the read's measured cost × the visible-dora factor
   (heuristic path; live play takes values from the network).
4. **Value-aware push budget**: a hand that is not tenpai may risk at most
   P(win) × value / (threat value × exposure turns); the same comparison lifts a slow,
   expensive hand (≥ 12 000) up to 25 %. Tenpai ≥ 12 000 may cut 20 %, ≥ 24 000 anything.
5. **Our yakuman**: `YakumanRoutes` values a kept hand that is shaped for one (daisangen,
   four winds, all honors, kokushi, suuankou at half, chinroutou, ryuuiisou, chuuren); a route
   switches that decision's ranking to point EV; the network chooses only among
   route-keeping tiles; a learned "pass" never declines a call that keeps a route; dama when
   every wait is already a yakuman.
6. **Their yakuman**: the tile that finishes two dragon sets or three wind sets gets a
   cost-equivalent danger (2.74 % × yakuman / the seat's usual value), so budget, betaori and
   expected cost all treat it as the 32 000 tile it is; pao is noted when it can still be pon'd.
7. **Profiles** (`OpponentProfiles`): novice-like seats (early middles ≥ 30 %, Beta prior at
   the Phoenix rate) lose the early-outside discount and the discard-only flush read; suji and
   honor tiles against them get a provisional ×1.3 (more non-ryanmen waits). All-last: a
   trailing riichi is worth at least what it needs to move up (capped at baiman).
8. **Instrumentation**: `dealin_calibration.csv` gains `read`, `tsumogiriStreak`, `profile`
   (an old-layout file is rotated aside); `win_offers.jsonl`.

Added after comparing V3 against the standard defensive curriculum (same corpus, second scan):

9. **Last safe tile**: from turn 6 with no riichi out, a hand holding exactly one safe tile
   (an honor with two copies out, or genbutsu to two opponents) keeps it unless discarding it
   costs nothing; the alternative pays 1.5 ukeire-equivalents (3 when far from tenpai).
10. **"Just got tenpai" cut**: a hand-discarded live dora, live yakuhai (at most one other copy
    out) or late middle tile is a likelihood ratio on the tenpai odds (`cut:*` cells). An
    early yakuhai cut is a normal shape cut and *lowers* the odds (Phoenix early tenpai 0.12 %
    after it vs 0.55 % after any other tile); a hand-cut dora raises them (1.41 %).
11. **Riichi tile**: same-suit tiles within three of the riichi tile scale by the measured ratio
    for their class, split by tedashi/tsumogiri (`rt-td±d|class`). The folklore "the riichi
    tile's neighbours are dangerous" does not hold: against a hand-cut riichi tile, a non-suji
    4-6 one away hits 8.9 % vs 9.8 % for that class overall.
12. **Ippatsu and houtei** are folded into the threat value (×1.35 while the riichi is on its
    ippatsu turn, ×1.3 on the last discard), so the budget, betaori and expected cost see them.
13. **Kokushi river** (closed, 8+ discards, at most one orphan thrown): tenpai floors at the
    measured rate (18 % mid, 19 % late) and terminals/honors become cost-equivalent dangers with
    the kokushi share priced as a yakuman (kokushi tenpai is 0.4 % of such seats mid-hand and
    2.6 % late, i.e. 2 % and 13 % of their tenpai hands).
14. **EV cap by profile**: the non-tenpai push cap applies against steady players only; against a
    novice-like primary threat the riichi tables' budget stands (§5).

## 3b. Per-player play (3.4)

The mixed-table arena (novices and Phoenix-level players at one table) showed V3 level with V2
while it gained in both pure fields, so 3.4 adapts per seat instead of per table.

**What a match can tell about a player** (`tools/seat_style_baseline.py`, 19 600 Phoenix
player-games): within one game a player's first half barely predicts the second (split-half
r: calls 0.05, early middles 0.045, folding to riichi 0.06, riichi 0.00). Per-player rates
would need ~70 hands to reach half weight; a match has 8-16. What differs live is the
*population*: on 2026-10-18 one human in three played like a novice (36 % early middles, a
6-of-6 hand that Phoenix players produce 0.1 % of the time, 22 % genbutsu after a riichi
against Phoenix's 67 %), the other two and all NPCs like Phoenix players.

1. **Two-type classifier** (`OpponentProfiles`): P(novice) per seat from per-hand likelihood
   ratios — middles among the first six discards (Phoenix histogram vs a beta-binomial novice,
   mean 35 %, concentration 2) and the genbutsu share after another's riichi in fifths
   (Phoenix vs a provisional novice centred near 35 %). Prior 25 %, tempered 0.7 for
   within-hand correlation. Phoenix player-games are misread as novice about 2 % of the time
   (archive n24, 5 000 games: 2.2 % after 8 hands, 2.6 % after 16). Today's tables: the
   novice-like human 0.97, everyone else 0.04-0.17.
2. **Seat budget**: a tile's danger is summed over every threat, weighted by tenpai × value
   against the primary's (`PushFoldDecision.Allows`). One threat: unchanged. Two equal riichi:
   the old ×0.5. A 30 % open hand behind a riichi counts at its share instead of being held to
   the riichi's line. The EV cap is lifted only when every threat reads novice-like.
3. **Dama against folders**: a good wait already worth 8 000 dama (12 000 as dealer) stays dama
   when no opponent reads novice-like; riichi's warning costs rons against players who fold.
4. **Instrumentation**: `hand_rivers.jsonl` (every seat's river with tsumogiri flags, global
   order, calls, riichi tile, outcome) and `tools/hand_rivers.py`, which recomputes the
   per-player table and P(novice) offline. The novice-side distributions are provisional
   until it has data.
5. **Arena**: `NoviceSeat.Casual` (calls 30 %, riichi 70 %, folds to riichi 35 % per discard)
   and a `live` field where each opponent is a casual novice one time in three, else the
   Phoenix-trained network.

## 3c. Blended reads (3.4)

The first 3.4 arena left V3 level with V2 on the `live` field, and three of its rules were
switches that one tile or one hand could flip: novice-like at P(novice) ≥ 50 %, a flush read at
an in-suit share of 15 %/20 %, a threat at tenpai ≥ 30 %. Two more were blanket overrides: no
safe tile pushed everything, and all-novice threats dropped the EV cap. 3.4 now treats each as
a quantity, keeping the rule-based tables as they are:

1. **Profile blend** (`ProfileBlend`): a seat's danger is the steady and the novice-like
   estimate mixed by P(novice). While the evidence is undecided it leans toward the higher one
   by `ProfileCaution` × 4q(1 − q) (0.5 by default): reading a novice as steady costs a deal-in,
   the reverse only a slower hand. An unprofiled seat (prior 25 %) gets about a third of the
   novice ×1.3 on its suji and honors, a Phoenix-like one after 8 hands almost none. Allowances
   that pay off only against novices go the other way: the EV cap moves toward the novice-table
   budget by the threats' exposure-weighted P(novice), shrunk by the same caution, and dama vs
   folders needs P(all three fold) = Π(1 − q) ≥ 50 %, which an unknown table (42 %) does not
   reach.
2. **Evidence decay** (`ProfileDecay` 0.9 per hand): a seat that changes how it plays is followed
   within a few hands. On archive n24 the mean P(novice) of Phoenix players after 16 hands goes
   from 4 % to 7 %; the live novice-like player stays at 75 %.
3. **Read mixture** (`ReadMixture`): a flush read from the river alone is a weight that runs
   from 1 (clean river) to 0 a tenth past the cutoff, and open-hand danger averages the table
   ratios of both reads. Two calls in one suit stay a certain flush. A novice-like reading uses
   the calls-only reads, so the blend in 1. also blends the reads.
4. **Every seat in the budget** (`ContinuousThreats`): seats below the 30 % floor add their
   tenpai × value share to a tile's summed danger. The floor still decides when defense starts
   and who the primary threat is. **Off by default**: it cost 0.05 placement on `live` and
   0.07 against novices (§5), folding to seats that are rarely tenpai.
5. **Retreat vs attack** (`RetreatEv`): with no safe tile the least costly discard is the
   retreat, and a tile may cost what the retreat costs plus the extra win value the attack buys
   over it, spread over `PushExposureTurns`. A cheap slow hand now folds with its least
   dangerous tile; a valuable one still pushes.

Yakuman handling (route valuation, route-keeping discards and calls, dama on an all-yakuman
wait, push with tenpai ≥ 24 000, the tile completing a visible daisangen/four winds) is its own
switch, `YakumanHandling`, on with Defense v2 as well: it is an attacking fix, and keeping it
behind V3 mixed it into every V2/V3 comparison. The kokushi river read stays a V3 read.

## 4. Validation

`Precompute arena <seeds> <novice|learned|mixed> <model|-> [arms] [threads] [hands]` plays

network. Results: §5.

the 30 % novice threshold (`tools/fit_danger.py` on the profile column).

Reading:

- **ContinuousThreats** is the one part with a clear effect, and it is harmful: it accounts for
  the whole V3 deficit on `live` and most of it against novices. A seat at 15 % tenpai costs
  little per tile but is present on every turn, so summing it in folds hands that should push.
  Off by default.
- **ProfileBlend and ReadMixture** are level on `live` (±0.007) and worth 0.017 placement /
  370 points against novices (with ContinuousThreats off). Kept. **RetreatEv** is level
  (0.007 better on `live`). Kept.
- **YakumanHandling** on V2 is a small, consistent gain (79 points a match, z 1.7): yakuman
  hands are rare, so the effect is small by construction, but it is never negative. On with V2.
- **V3 against V2.** With these defaults V3 is level on `live` (+0.003) and against the
  Phoenix-trained network (−0.015, measured with it on), and still behind against novices (+0.044, as at 54a4356).
  It deals in less on every field (live 8.2 → 7.2 %, learned 9.8 → 8.5 %, novice 2.5 → 2.0 %)
  but wins less: the defense is real, and against these fields it is not worth what it costs
  in attack. The release rule was V3 ≥ V2 on `live`; it does not clear it, so **3.4 ships with
  V2 as the default and V3 as an opt-in setting**, and the yakuman fixes reach every player
  through V2.

What would move V3: the arena novices never fold and riichi any wait, so they reward a pure
attacker; humans fold. A live A/B (auto play with `hand_rivers.jsonl`) is the test that matters
before V3 becomes the default.

## 6. Calibration and table reads (branch `defense-table-reads`)

The arena knows every hidden hand, so `Precompute calibrate` compares what the candidate's
opponent model believes with the truth at each of its discards (100 seeds a field, guarded V3).

**What was wrong** (before any change):

| read | Phoenix-trained seats | casual seats (live field) | arena novices |
|---|---|---|---|
| tenpai, 1 / 2 calls, predicted vs true | 26 / 54 % vs 29 / 52 % | 26 / 55 % vs 5.5 / 14 % | 26 / 57 % vs 5.5 / 15 % |
| open-hand ron value, true / predicted | 0.73-0.78 | 0.68-0.73 | 0.56 |
| our riichi tenpai, predicted vs won | 34 % vs 45 % | — | 31 % vs 78 % (three novices) |
| genbutsu share after another riichi | 72.7 % | 58.4 % | 46.0 % (never folds: chance) |

Live FFXIV draws agree in direction: two-call seats were tenpai at 49 % of draws against 86 %
predicted (`tenpai_calibration.csv`, 69 rows). Riichi and dama values are on target, and V2's
danger given tenpai is calibrated.

**Reading seat by seat does not work in a 4-hand match.** An opponent makes about three
discards after a riichi per match and shows its hand at about one draw, and the novice
classifier stays near its prior (25-29 % for arena novices). A per-seat "is this opponent
pushing" term in our win estimate fitted to exactly zero on 3 590 arena tenpai. What does work
is reading the *population* across matches:

- **TenpaiReveals.** Every exhaustive draw labels each non-riichi opponent with the model's
  last estimate, call group (closed, 1, 2, 3+), looseness (1 − fold read) and the shown tenpai.
  A logistic correction per group (MAP, normal priors) plus a per-seat offset from this
  match's draws. Persisted live in `tenpai_reveals.csv`, per policy.
- **WinReveals.** Our first tenpai of each hand and whether the hand was ours fit a factor on
  the win estimate's K (closed and open). Persisted live in `win_reveals.csv`.
- **OpenValueScale 0.75** for open, non-riichi hands.

In the arena an arm with reveals first plays 200 warm-up matches on other seeds against the
same field. Learned corrections (logit, closed / 1 / 2 / 3+ calls; win factor closed / open):
novice −2.3 / −2.5 / −2.7 / −2.4, ×3.15 / ×1.90; live −0.0 / −1.0 / −1.2 / −1.1, ×1.43 / ×1.32;
Phoenix-trained +0.6 / 0.0 / −0.6 / −0.3, ×1.07 / ×1.12. After the novice warm-up, novice
tenpai reads 3.7 / 11.5 / 35 % at 1 / 2 / 3+ calls against a true 5 / 14 / 41 %; the
Phoenix-trained field stays calibrated. On the mixed live field the population correction
averages the two kinds of seat: casual two-call seats 30 % vs 10 % true, Phoenix-trained 31 %
vs 55 %.

value scale the rest; the win factor (×1.43 closed on live) changes little.

**Warm-up needed** (live, V3 + reads vs V3): 0 matches −0.010 ± 0.014 (empty store: only the
value scale and this match's seat offsets), 25 matches −0.049 ± 0.024 (335 revealed seats,
coefficients already near the 200-match fit), 200 matches −0.056 ± 0.024. A live install pays
off after a few dozen matches; the arena freezes the store after warm-up, while live keeps
learning.
