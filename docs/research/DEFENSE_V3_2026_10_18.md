# Defense v3 and yakuman handling (2026-10-18)

Goal: protect better against Novice-queue players (random-looking discards, big open hands)
and against strong players steering the hand, and stop losing yakuman. Everything below is
behind `DefenseModel.V3` (settings: "Defense model v3 (testing)"); V2 and Legacy are kept for A/B.

## 1. What was wrong

### Yakuman

`YakuDetector` and the win gate were **not** the problem. A probe (all twelve Doman yakuman,
ron and tsumo, after kans, open daisangen, 13-sided kokushi, confirmed and unconfirmed
windows) detected and declared every one. What failed was everything before the win:

| Gap | Where | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A hand that is not tenpai is worth dora + 1 han (~1 500) | `HandValue.EstimatePoints` | a yakuman 1-shanten folds to any riichi like trash |
| The tenpai budget tops out at "cut a 10 % tile" (12 %) | `PushFoldPolicy.Decide` | a 48 000 tenpai still folds a 15 % tile |
| Open hands bypass the network and rank by tile efficiency | `LearnedDiscardPolicy` → `HandAnalyzer` | East triplet + 6z pon + three wind pairs threw a wind pair to keep 5m-6m |
| Riichi on a yakuman tenpai | `RiichiPolicy` vetoes on shape only | warns the table for no extra value |
| No defence against a visible yakuman | danger model | the third dragon into two dragon sets was an ordinary honor |

The lost live win (auto play, logs rotated) could not be reconstructed; `win_offers.jsonl`
now records every offer, decision, dispatch and outcome so the next one cannot vanish.

### Defense

Live logs to 2026-09-25: 11 deal-ins in 310 hands, but three cost 12 000-13 000 — one a 19 %
half-suji pushed at turn 14 into a two-call open hand rated 72 % tenpai. 2026-10-18 (human
match): a 2-shanten worth 1 000 ran a 7 % budget against the dealer's two-call ~10 000,
loosened by the tenpai division and a ×1.39 placement factor — nothing compared what the
push could win with what it risked.

The danger model applied riichi tables to every seat, so open hands were read wrong in both
directions (below).

## 3. What V3 does

1. **Hand reads** (`HandRead`, rules identical to the counter): flush (suit), toitoi, yakuhai,
   tanyao, other. Against a non-riichi seat, danger = the riichi-table estimate (kabe, live
   suji, dora intact) × corpus rate(read, class) / corpus rate(riichi, class)
   (`OpenDangerTable.DangerRatio`, clamped 0.05-4). Dama seats use the dama row.
2. **Tsumogiri tell**: the trailing run is a likelihood ratio on the tenpai odds, applied to
   the logistic estimate and to the network's tenpai head (`TenpaiEstimator.WithTsumogiri`).
3. **Threat value**: open hands priced from the read's measured cost × the visible-dora factor
   (heuristic path; live play takes values from the network).
4. **Value-aware push budget**: a hand that is not tenpai may risk at most
   P(win) × value / (threat value × exposure turns); the same comparison lifts a slow,
   expensive hand (≥ 12 000) up to 25 %. Tenpai ≥ 12 000 may cut 20 %, ≥ 24 000 anything.
5. **Our yakuman**: `YakumanRoutes` values a kept hand that is shaped for one (daisangen,
   four winds, all honors, kokushi, suuankou at half, chinroutou, ryuuiisou, chuuren); a route
   switches that decision's ranking to point EV; the network chooses only among
   route-keeping tiles; a learned "pass" never declines a call that keeps a route; dama when
   every wait is already a yakuman.
6. **Their yakuman**: the tile that finishes two dragon sets or three wind sets gets a
   cost-equivalent danger (2.74 % × yakuman / the seat's usual value), so budget, betaori and
   expected cost all treat it as the 32 000 tile it is; pao is noted when it can still be pon'd.
7. **Profiles** (`OpponentProfiles`): novice-like seats (early middles ≥ 30 %, Beta prior at
   the Phoenix rate) lose the early-outside discount and the discard-only flush read; suji and
   honor tiles against them get a provisional ×1.3 (more non-ryanmen waits). All-last: a
   trailing riichi is worth at least what it needs to move up (capped at baiman).
8. **Instrumentation**: `dealin_calibration.csv` gains `read`, `tsumogiriStreak`, `profile`
   (an old-layout file is rotated aside); `win_offers.jsonl`.

Added after comparing V3 against the standard defensive curriculum (same corpus, second scan):

9. **Last safe tile**: from turn 6 with no riichi out, a hand holding exactly one safe tile
   (an honor with two copies out, or genbutsu to two opponents) keeps it unless discarding it
   costs nothing; the alternative pays 1.5 ukeire-equivalents (3 when far from tenpai).
10. **"Just got tenpai" cut**: a hand-discarded live dora, live yakuhai (at most one other copy
    out) or late middle tile is a likelihood ratio on the tenpai odds (`cut:*` cells). An
    early yakuhai cut is a normal shape cut and *lowers* the odds (Phoenix early tenpai 0.12 %
    after it vs 0.55 % after any other tile); a hand-cut dora raises them (1.41 %).
11. **Riichi tile**: same-suit tiles within three of the riichi tile scale by the measured ratio
    for their class, split by tedashi/tsumogiri (`rt-td±d|class`). The folklore "the riichi
    tile's neighbours are dangerous" does not hold: against a hand-cut riichi tile, a non-suji
    4-6 one away hits 8.9 % vs 9.8 % for that class overall.
12. **Ippatsu and houtei** are folded into the threat value (×1.35 while the riichi is on its
    ippatsu turn, ×1.3 on the last discard), so the budget, betaori and expected cost see them.
13. **Kokushi river** (closed, 8+ discards, at most one orphan thrown): tenpai floors at the
    measured rate (18 % mid, 19 % late) and terminals/honors become cost-equivalent dangers with
    the kokushi share priced as a yakuman (kokushi tenpai is 0.4 % of such seats mid-hand and
    2.6 % late, i.e. 2 % and 13 % of their tenpai hands).
14. **EV cap by profile**: the non-tenpai push cap applies against steady players only; against a
    novice-like primary threat the riichi tables' budget stands (§5).

## 4. Validation

`Precompute arena <seeds> <novice|learned|mixed> <model|-> [arms] [threads] [hands]` plays
duplicate matches (each seed four times with the candidate rotated through the seats) against
simulated Novice-queue players (`NoviceSeat`: tile efficiency only, never folds, calls any
yaku-keeping meld 75 %, riichi on any wait, 35 % random discards) or the Phoenix-trained
network. Results: §5.

Provisional numbers to fit from live data once rows accumulate: the novice ×1.3 factor and
the 30 % novice threshold (`tools/fit_danger.py` on the profile column).
